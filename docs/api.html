The model¶

State-space model of one athlete’s physiology, as a log posterior over everything unknown.

States (daily, unconstrained): a chain of ceilings - log MAP (the aerobic ceiling: 5-min power, about a ramp test’s MAP), logit f_T (CP / MAP), logit f_E (LT1 / 1-h power) - plus log Pmax, log durability (fade = 1 / durability, see PowerCurve) and a fatigue F (hours at CP-equivalent). CP = f_T MAP, LT1 = f_E P(1 h), so LT1 < 1-h power < CP < MAP always (LT1 as a share of CP passed 1-h power, 2026-11-01); W’ is what puts the power-duration curve’s 5-min power at MAP. Severe / VO2max work raises the ceiling (and CP and LT1 with it); threshold work fills CP toward MAP and endurance fills LT1 toward CP, each saturating as its share nears 1.

Each day:

  • the day’s power histogram is split into domains by the current LT1, CP, MAP and a sprint line (soft edges), so “threshold work” moves up as you get fitter; plus hours past 2 h in a ride (long). The band LT1-MAP splits into heavy (z2) and severe (z3) by power and heart rate together (v16): a second’s severe share is logistic((log P - log CP) / EDGE_WIDTH + e), e the heart-rate evidence (metrics.severe_hr: corrected heart rate above threshold heart rate, in units of its scatter) - 0 without heart rate (planned sessions too), so the plain power edge. Power alone can’t tell steady from severe within a few % of CP; heart rate alone counted long hot rides and climbs (PhysioData.hr_hist_h, EVIDENCE);

  • dose -> an adaptation pool per state (gain matrix: which domain trains which state); the pool turns into capacity at rate rho * exp(-gamma F): you get stronger while recovering, slower while tired - the cost of fatigue is learned, not a set limit;

  • capacity decays toward a detrained floor with its own time constant (TRAINED states only);

  • Pmax and durability are slowly drifting levels (process noise only, no gains, no detraining): the data (no all-out sprints or long all-out efforts) can’t say how they respond to training - with gains, those chains didn’t mix (R-hat 2, 2026-10-25). Their floor / tau entries are then prior only;

  • fatigue F builds with load sum_bins hours * (P / CP)^2 and clears with time constant tau_F; it lowers what you can do all-out by exp(-s_F F) (not capacity);

  • process noise every NOISE_WEEKS weeks (non-centred, fixed size NOISE_SD per week, scaled by sqrt(NOISE_WEEKS) so the drift per unit time is the same) on each state: what the model doesn’t explain. A period’s change comes in equal parts on its days, not as one step. An illness’s weeks get a larger size (PhysioData.noise_scale): unaware of one, the fit read the drop as fast detraining (2026-11-11). Its size is fixed, not fitted: with a fitted size the posterior mode runs off (big noise, every reading matched exactly). One term a week shared by all states was tried (2026-11-09): near-max power forecast as well, but LT1 and CP/MAP could then only move in step with MAP - the fit ran to 6-day detraining and put LT1 45 % below alpha1 (into its bias).

Observations:

  • ride best powers, censored - most rides aren’t all-out. A mixture: all-out (Gaussian in log power, sd ALL_OUT_SD, around the fatigued curve) or not (log deficit below the capacity curve ~ Gamma, one shape; its mean a straight line in log duration (log), plus a step per ride kind: easy / hard / test-race - by mean and shape, not scale and shape: those two slid along a ridge, correlation -0.94). Deficits are measured from capacity so an easy day after a hard one reads as a choice, not as fatigue. Either way a small fixed share (BAD_READING_SHARE) may be bad readings, spread evenly in log power: one broken power meter’s best no longer drags the curve to it;

  • threshold readings (your FTP entries, alpha1, ramp tests) with a fitted bias and error per source;

  • steady stretches (rides, >= 5-min reps): power at their heart rate. log power = log CP + g(HR / LTHR) + an offset for reps + c F + error: g is the rider’s own heart-rate-to-power curve (HR_KNOTS, linear between), c how fatigue holds heart rate down (tired: more watts at the same HR), the error partly shared within a week (heat, hydration, sleep), integrated out (hr_terms). Replaces scaling each ride’s power to threshold HR along a straight line from resting HR (2026-11-09): that read 0 % low from easy rides and 11 % low from tempo rides, so a change in the week’s ride mix looked like a change in CP, and the fit made adaptation near-instant to follow it.

Recovery markers (sleep, HRV, resting HR, bedtime; PhysioData.recovery_z) are not in the model (2026-10-26): as causes of recovery they fitted backwards (you sleep more because you are tired); as daily signals of fatigue (with fatigue noise) they swamped everything - thousands of strongly autocorrelated days turned fatigue into a slow drift (tau_F 115-340 d). They act through readiness instead; a measurement model on weekly averages is the next thing to try.

Priors are Normal on the unconstrained values, so the negative log posterior is minimised directly (MAP) and sampled with NUTS on the same vector.

class goodpush.physio.curve.PowerCurve[source]¶

Omni-domain power-duration model (Puchowicz, Baker & Clarke 2020):

P(t) = W’/t * (1 - exp(-t (Pmax - CP) / W’)) + CP t <= 30 min

… - fade * CP * ln(t / 30 min) t > 30 min

Short end -> Pmax, middle -> CP + W’/t, long end falls by fade x CP per e-fold of duration past 30 min (durability: a durable rider has a small fade). All arguments broadcast.

classmethod power(t, cp, w_prime, pmax, fade)[source]¶
classmethod w_prime_for(t, p, cp, pmax)[source]¶

W’ that puts P(t) at p (t <= 30 min): W’/t (1 - exp(-t (Pmax - CP)/W’)) = p - CP. With y = t (Pmax - CP)/W’ that is (1 - e^-y)/y = rho := (p - CP)/(Pmax - CP), solved for y by Newton steps in log y from y = 1/rho (exact as rho -> 0); rho is kept in (0.001, 0.95) (p between CP and Pmax).

Fitting: MAP (minimum negative log posterior, L-BFGS) and uncertainty by NUTS samples (exact in the limit; about half an hour on years of data). A Laplace approximation (a Gaussian at the mode) was tried and removed (2026-11-10): on real data its draws were worth about 1 in 200 by importance sampling. The potential, its gradient and the Hessian for the preconditioner come from NumbaPosterior (hand-written backward pass; the JAX potential stays the reference it is tested against).

Both work on standardised parameters w = (z - prior mean) / prior sd: every coordinate on the same scale, so the first L-BFGS steps are sane and NUTS starts with a sensible mass matrix.

class goodpush.physio.fit.GainShares(model, log_dose)[source]¶

NUTS coordinates for the training effects: per state, the log total effect at the average dose mix and the logit shares of its dose domains, instead of one log effect per domain. The data pin down what the usual mix of training does, not each domain alone (z1 and z2 mostly come together), a curved ridge in log effects that NUTS crawled along (2026-11-01: ESS 4-8 of 400 for f_E’s gains). The map is one to one and the potential is Jacobian-corrected: the same posterior, other coordinates. For the LEVEL states the total is the response after HORIZON days of the usual dose instead of the long-run one, r = log total + log(1 - exp(-HORIZON / tau)), and the detrained floor’s entry is the level there, floor + exp(r). For a short tau r is the long-run effect, for a long one the first gain x HORIZON: what the data pin down in both cases. With the long-run total the chains froze for 25-200 draws at long time constants, where a small step in log tau moved the total and the floor a long way (2026-11-12). Years of rides fix that level; how it splits into floor and training lift they barely say, and with the lift a log coordinate that ridge is curved (2026-11-12: 1.8 % divergent draws, most of them where MAP’s lift was large and its floor low). Both are shears (r moves by a function of log tau, the level by one of r): their Jacobian is 1. v: standardised w with the log-effect entries replaced by these coordinates, the LEVEL states’ floors by levels.

class goodpush.physio.fit.Whitening(v_mode, stages=())[source]¶

The linear map NUTS samples through: v = v_mode + L u, NUTS on u with an identity mass. L = L_1 L_2 … L_k, each stage L_i = diag(s_i) (I + V_i diag(c_i) V_i^T) with V_i orthonormal columns (low rank: a leapfrog step costs D x rank, not D^2). The first stage is the inverse Hessian’s square root at the mode (from_hessian); each warm-up block adds one learned from that block’s draws and gradients (refined). The mode’s curvature is local: a long or curved ridge, or a region the mode doesn’t see, is wider than it says. Learning from where the chains go catches that in every run, whatever the model or rider (2026-11-09: ~15 directions 10-28x wider than the Hessian said - start level, floor and training effect trading off - put two chains at the tree limit, 1023 steps a draw). Draws have covariance S and gradients S^-1: too wide directions stand out in the draws, too narrow ones in the gradients (refined).

Model-predictive weekly load: how much to ride each week so the model’s forecast is best on the days that matter, within your hours.

The week’s sessions come from outside (the plan’s template: its key sessions, tests, races, which days are easy). Per week two numbers are chosen:

  • easy hours e_w (spread over the week’s easy days, the long day weighted LONG_WEIGHT),

  • key scale s_w in [0, 1]: how much of the week’s key sessions to keep (1 = all of them, less = an unloading week).

Forecast = PhysioModel.forecast from the last fitted day, no process noise. How much fatigue costs - slower adaptation, less on the day - is learned from your data, not a limit (your sleep / HRV / resting HR / bedtime act through readiness, day to day). Objective (minimised):

  • sum over targets of weight x mean log power over the target’s durations (all-out with fatigue, or capacity), + TIME_COST per hour ridden, + FATIGUE_PENALTY x (fatigue above the backstop)^2 (the highest fatigue in your history: never planned beyond what you have been through), + KEEP_WEIGHT x (1 - s_w)^2 (keep the template’s key sessions unless the forecast gains), + RAMP_WEIGHT x (block shape violated, hours)^2: within a block each loading week at least RAMP_MIN more hours than the week before, the recovery week at most RECOVERY_MAX of the week before (the block structure you train in; the model picks the levels), + STEADY_WEIGHT x (hours short of the week’s steady hours)^2: time on the bike grows steadily (the volume ramp: what you have been riding, more after each loading week) unless the forecast clearly gains otherwise. A week far from the target days barely moves the forecast - without this its hours would be wherever the optimiser stops.

Smooth in (e, s), so L-BFGS-B with box bounds and JAX gradients; a few dozen variables, < 1 s.

The loss is compiled once for the class, with the problem’s arrays as an argument; days, weeks and targets are padded up to buckets (padding has no load, no weight and is masked out), so a re-plan with a day less to go or another phase reuses the compiled loss instead of compiling a new one.

Your watts for a session’s targets: textbook %FTP mapped through your own thresholds.

The workout library writes targets as fractions of FTP (endurance 0.55-0.65, VO2max 1.08-1.15, …), which assumes everyone’s LT1 is 75 % of FTP, MAP 120 % and so on. Here each fraction becomes watts by straight lines between your thresholds on the session’s day (the physio model’s forecast for that day, or today’s values):

%FTP 0 0.75 1.00 1.20 1.50 2.50 watts 0 LT1 CP MAP (5 min) REPS_1MIN x 1-min SPRINT x Pmax

So endurance stays below your LT1 wherever it is, threshold sits at your CP, VO2max between CP and your MAP, repeated 1-min efforts at 80 % of what you could do all-out for a minute. Sessions are stored in textbook %FTP (accepted versions don’t churn as the thresholds move); this map turns them into watts wherever watts are shown or exported. Forecasts change as the model updates with new rides.
