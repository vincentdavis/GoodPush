Methods in short¶

How GoodPush gets its numbers, outside the physiological model (which has its own pages). The papers and books behind each method are listed in References and in the app: Fitness > Sources.

All of GoodPush’s own findings about you (what drove your gains, illness links, how fast you can ramp) come from your data alone: n = 1 and observational, so read them as hypotheses to test, not proof.

  • Ride streams: resampled to one sample a second. Gaps of 10 s or less are filled forward, gaps of 10-300 s are stops (0 W), longer gaps are cut.

  • Per ride: normalized power, IF, TSS, best powers from 1 s to 2 h, time in power and heart-rate zones, efficiency factor (NP / HR) and aerobic decoupling [Allen et al., 2019, Friel, 2018]. Rides without power use heart-rate TSS.

  • FTP (automatic): fitted to Garmin’s own FTP estimates and your entered tests, as a weighted mix of your best 3-60 min powers (older efforts fading) and your power at threshold heart rate on steady rides. With too few of those to fit, it uses critical power [Jones et al., 2010, Monod and Scherrer, 1965] from the last 90 days instead. [ftp] mode = "manual" uses your tests.

  • Workout types: each ride is classed as recovery, endurance, long, tempo, sweet spot, threshold, VO2max, anaerobic, race or test, from its sustained efforts relative to FTP, checked against heart rate and the workout’s name.

  • Load: CTL (42-day), ATL (7-day), TSB (form) [Allen et al., 2019, Banister et al., 1975], the acute:chronic ratio [Gabbett, 2016, Hulin et al., 2016], monotony and strain [Foster, 1998], ramp rate [Friel, 2018].

  • Performance marker: the best 20-minute-equivalent power on hard days in the previous 6 weeks. Weeks without hard riding are “untested”, not “detrained”. Aerobic efficiency on steady rides is a second marker that needs no hard efforts.

  • What drove your progress: correlations between 8-week training features (volume, intensity, consistency, rest, sleep…) and the change in the marker, with a cross-validated model as a check on how much they predict.

  • Overreaching [Aubry et al., 2014, Meeusen et al., 2013]: weeks with load warning signs (a sharp load jump, deep fatigue, high monotony), each judged by what happened in the next 6 weeks (performance, a forced break, resting HR, HRV, sleep, fatigue notes).

  • Illness: sickness words in ride names and descriptions (flu, covid, cough, sinus…) mark comeback rides; each illness is rebuilt backwards from the note (the training gap plus a resting-HR rise), then checked for warning signs in the weeks before. Add or correct illnesses as [[events]] type = "illness".

  • Fitness-fatigue model: your personal fitness and fatigue time constants (Banister), fitted to your tested performances [Banister et al., 1975, Calvert et al., 1976]; from one athlete’s data they are poorly identified [Hellard et al., 2006].

DFA alpha1¶

If you record the alphaHRV Connect IQ data field, or ride with GoodPush’s trainer bar and a chest strap, GoodPush reads alpha1 (how organised your beat-to-beat rhythm is: about 1.0 easy, falling through 0.75 near LT1 and 0.5 near LT2; Gronwald et al., 2020, Peng et al., 1995, Rogers et al., 2021, Rogers et al., 2021).

  • Warm-up check: each ride’s alpha1 and heart rate in the warm-up (minutes 5-20) and as the session gets going (20-40) against what your last 6 weeks say they should be at the same power, from steady riding with clean data only. Clearly lower or higher than normal is flagged; a warm-up well off normal makes the next day amber.

  • Thresholds: LT1 (alpha1 0.75) and LT2 (0.5) with your heart rate at each. In ordinary rides alpha1 also falls with time into a ride, so these read low; a ramp test (power climbing steadily for 10+ minutes to above FTP, then stopping) gives better ones, and GoodPush spots ramp tests in your rides by itself.

  • VLamax [Mader and Heck, 1986] can’t be estimated from power alone (it needs blood lactate); a glycolytic profile is shown instead: your best 15 / 30 / 60 s power relative to CP and W’.
