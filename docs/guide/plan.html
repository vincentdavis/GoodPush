The training plan¶

GoodPush plans every day of your phases, from today to the end of the last one, and re-plans as your rides and mornings come in. Turn it on in Settings > Plan ([plan] enabled = true). The app shows it on Today, Plan and the Calendar; it is also written to reports/plan/:

File

What it is

plan.md

today’s readiness, the sessions with watt targets, the week targets

plan.ics

a calendar file: import it into Google / Apple / Outlook calendar (re-importing updates the same events)

workouts/gp_*.fit, gp_*.zwo

structured workouts for a Garmin watch or bike computer, and for Zwift / Incyclist

plan.json

the same plan for other tools

How to ride it (watch, Incyclist, GoodPush’s own trainer bar): Riding the plan.

Phases and races¶

Plan > Phases & races (stored in data/plans.json). A phase has a type, a start and a length; phases can’t overlap; gaps are allowed and nothing is planned in them.

Phase

What it plans

Base

endurance with sweet spot and tempo, no VO2max

Build

threshold every week, VO2max / sweet spot alternating by block

Specialty

threshold + VO2max, tapers into your races

Maintenance

one quality session a week, fitness held

Recovery / downtime

easy riding only, about half your hours

Each phase has a cadence: loading weeks before a recovery week (3 = 3:1, 2 = 2:1, 0 = none) and optionally its own weekly hours.

Races have a date, an A / B / C priority, a type (road, crit, tt, mtb, gravel, cx, hillclimb, fondo) and an optional series; paste several at once, one per line: 2026-11-29, B, crit, Round 1. A race day gets the race, the day before openers, the day after an easy spin. An A race gets a taper of about two weeks (the week before 70 %, its week 50 % of the load, intensity kept; Mujika and Padilla, 2003), a B race a lighter week; no key session 1-2 days before an A race and no tests in race weeks.

Phases suggested from your races. When your A races need weeks that none of your phases cover, the Plan page shows “Suggested from your races”, planned back from each A race the way coaches periodise [Friel, 2018]: Specialty for the 8 weeks up to the race, Build before it, Base before that, recovery weeks after the last A race. Your own phases always win. “Add these phases” adds them; “Not now” hides them until your races or phases change.

Your time¶

Settings > Plan: hours per weekday (Mon..Sun). The longest day gets the long ride, a 0 day is rest, no session is longer than its day. hours_per_week caps the week. The plan builds up to it gradually: a week at most 10 % above your average week of the last 4, one ride at most 10 % above your longest recent ride.

Settings¶

[plan] in goodpush.toml; the ones marked * are also in the app (Settings > Plan).

Days off (with availability: the 0 days).

["tue", "thu"]

The two days for hard sessions; tests go on the first.

long_day *

"sat"

The long ride, only used without availability.

recovery_days *

["fri"]

Easy spin days.

intensity *

"model"

What the hard days are: "model" (the physiological model picks each key day’s session for the best forecast on your race mornings; pyramidal until it’s fitted), "polarized" (easy below LT1 plus hard work above LT2 only; Seiler and Kjerland, 2006, Stöggl and Sperlich, 2014) or "pyramidal" (tempo and sweet spot in base, threshold and VO2max in build and specialty). Every style has the same number of hard days: 2 a week in base, build and specialty, 1 in maintenance and recovery weeks.

mix_draws

10

With intensity = "model": how many of the model’s possible versions of you the choice is averaged over (robust to what your data barely pins down); 0 = the best fit alone (faster).

load_model *

"ctl"

How much to ride each week. "physio": the physiological model picks each week’s hours and hard work for the best forecast on your race mornings, learns from your data how much fatigue costs you, and reads your sleep / HRV / resting HR as signs of fatigue (needs about 4 months of rides). "ctl": fixed rules (min_ramp / max_ramp, recovery weeks at 70 %, tapers by form).

min_ramp, max_ramp

1.5, 2.5

"ctl" only: how many CTL points a loading week adds, at least / at most.

test_every_weeks

6

A 20-min FTP test when the last one is older than this.

ramp_every_weeks

6

A ramp test with alphaHRV for LT1 / LT2 (0 = never).

ftp *

0

0 = the FTP from your data; a number overrides it for the plan.

How it decides¶

  • Weekly load. With load_model = "physio" the model’s forecast decides it. With "ctl", one optimisation over all weeks of your phases sets each week’s load: base and build weeks raise CTL between min_ramp and max_ramp a week, recovery weeks are about 70 % of a steady week, everything fits your hours, and races set the taper (form +10..+25 on an A-race morning, at least 0 for a B race).

  • Daily sessions. A second optimisation picks each day’s session (rest, recovery spin, endurance of any length, or the phase’s quality types) to hit the week’s load within your hours. Two neighbouring days’ hardness add to at most 3 (VO2 / threshold = 2, tempo / sweet spot = 1, easy = 0), so no two hard days in a row; at least one rest day in a full week; form (TSB) stays above -30 and a hard day needs at least -20. It sticks to what you accepted unless a rule or your readiness needs a change.

  • Interval progression. Each type (threshold, VO2max, sweet spot, tempo, anaerobic, a durability finish on long rides, Maunder et al., 2021) has a ladder of formats and levels (4x5 → … → 2x25 min threshold; 30/15s, step-down and over-under VO2max sessions…). Each session’s note says what it trains. A session on target (at least 90 % of its work time on target) moves you a step up; partly or above target repeats the step; fell short goes one step back. Two weeks without a type’s intervals take its level a step down, then one more every 10 days. Set a level yourself on the Plan page (Interval progression).

  • Push week. The last loading week of a base or build block aims to end at form about -20: tired enough to want the recovery week, well short of overreaching [Aubry et al., 2014, Meeusen et al., 2013].

  • Adapting. A missed key session is made up on a free easy day later that week; after an unplanned hard ride the next key session moves back. If your threshold and sweet-spot sessions keep landing 3 % or more above target, it proposes FTP +3 % (well short twice: -3 %).

  • You decide. Once a day is planned, a real change to it is a proposal you accept or reject on the Calendar. Until you accept, the plan, watch files and Incyclist keep what you had. Small volume tweaks, new days and readiness changes go straight in.

  • Morning readiness (from Garmin plus your load; Fullagar et al., 2015, Plews et al., 2013):

    • red: resting HR +3 bpm two mornings running, HRV well below normal, or under 5.5 h sleep. Today and tomorrow become rest or easy spins.

    • amber: low HRV, short sleep, a 3-night sleep debt, form below -30, or a recent sickness note in a ride. No hard session today (tempo / sweet spot at most) and a test is postponed. After a run of 4 of the last 7 mornings not green, tomorrow stays easy too.

Targets in your watts¶

With load_model = "physio", sessions are designed in textbook % of FTP and then mapped through your own thresholds on the session’s day: 75 % → your LT1, 100 % → CP, 120 % → MAP (5-min power), 150 % → 80 % of an all-out minute, 250 % → 90 % of your sprint, straight lines between. Endurance stays below your LT1 wherever it is, VO2max work sits between your CP and MAP. Later sessions use the model’s forecast for their day. The Plan page shows today’s anchors.

The physiological model¶

The model (Rider and Model pages, and the plan with load_model = "physio") is fitted to your whole history: its best fit takes about a minute, and the app keeps it current after each re-plan in about a second.

Its ranges (the 5-95 % bands) come from a longer run that draws thousands of possible versions of you (NUTS sampling, Hoffman and Gelman, 2014). Start it once from a terminal; it saves itself as it goes, so you can stop it (Ctrl-C or close the window) and run the same command again to carry on:

goodpush physio --nuts --samples 2500 --ess 400 --max-minutes 540

It usually finishes within an hour, 9 hours at most. After that the app refreshes the ranges by itself about once a week, in the background at low priority ([physio] ranges_every_days, 0 = never; Settings > Physiological model). A full run is only needed again when the model’s version changes.

Everything the model finds is about one person’s data and observational: treat its training responses as hypotheses. How it works: the model pages.
