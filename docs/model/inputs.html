Inputs¶

The model sees plain arrays (goodpush.physio.PhysioData), built from goodpush’s tables by goodpush.models.physio.PhysioInputs. Three kinds of data go in.

Training (known inputs, not modelled)¶

  • Daily power histograms: hours per 10 W bin (0-1500 W, more in the top bin), summed over the day’s rides; coasting (the 0 W bin) left out. Rides without power contribute their heart-rate zone seconds, placed at fixed fractions of that day’s FTP (0.50, 0.60, 0.75, 0.88, 0.98, 1.08, 1.20 for zones 1-7).

  • Long-ride hours: per day, the hours past 2 h in each ride, summed.

The training is taken as exactly known: power meter error and rides missing from the record are not modelled.

Ride best powers (observations of capacity)¶

For every day with a ride, the best power over each of 12 durations: 5, 15, 30 s, 1, 3, 5, 10, 20, 40, 60, 90 and 120 min (only durations the ride lasted, only rides with power for more than 90 % of their time). Each day also has a ride kind, the hardest of its rides: easy; hard (a threshold, VO2max, anaerobic, sweet-spot or tempo workout, or a race); test. A race is a hard ride, not a test: its best powers come inside repeated efforts, not from fresh steady ones. The kind matters for how likely a best power is all-out (see Likelihood).

A best power whose ride shows it went all-out is maxed, whatever the day’s kind: heart rate around the effort’s end (1 min: the 45 s after it, heart rate lagging a short effort; 3 to 20 min: the effort’s last 15 to 60 s) came within 10 bpm (1 min), 7 (3 min) or 5 (5 to 20 min) of your maximum heart rate then - the 99th percentile of your rides’ highest heart rates in the year around it, as it drifts with age and fitness - and an effort of 3 min or more was steady (at most 5 % of its seconds without power). These are the measurements of the curve; FTP, CP and W’ are inferred from it (metrics.effort_hr.EffortHeartRate).

Most best powers are not all-out, so they are censored observations: they say capacity was at least this high.

Power that is wrong is set aside before the model sees it (metrics.power_check.PowerCheck):

  • rides you mark (the Ride page’s “Power is wrong”, or goodpush.toml [ingest] bad_power: ride ids, days or date ranges - a meter that read high for weeks) count as rides without power: their time and heart rate stay, their load comes from heart rate, and they give no best powers, threshold watts or steady stretches;

  • a ride whose best of 3 to 20 min is more than 1.2 x your rides’ 99th percentile at that length (the next-highest ride’s, so a glitch never sets its own bar) while heart rate around its end stayed under 0.95 x threshold heart rate loses its best powers - minutes far above your usual at an easy heart rate are a broken meter, not an effort. Its time in each power zone stays. [ingest] keep_power lists rides to leave alone.

What gets past both is the likelihood’s bad-reading share (Likelihood).

Threshold readings (observations of thresholds)¶

Each reading measures one quantity (LT1, CP, MAP or 1-h power) through a source with its own bias and error, both fitted (priors in Priors):

Source

Measures

Where it comes from

my_ftp

CP

the FTP history you entered ([ftp] history)

alpha1_lt1

LT1

power where DFA alpha1 crosses 0.75 on ordinary rides (HRVT1, Rogers et al. [2021])

ramp_lt1, ramp_lt2

LT1, CP

from ramp-to-failure tests: alpha1 = 0.75 and 0.5 [Rogers et al., 2021, Rogers et al., 2021] - upper bounds (Likelihood). The ramp’s top is no reading: the ride’s best powers are efforts like any other

Garmin’s own estimates (its FTP, its VO2max) are not readings: the analysis stands on your rides, tests and entries alone. Garmin’s FTP is shown for comparison only.

FTP-type readings are taken to measure CP (a threshold), not an all-out hour. Threshold heart rate is an input (your setting or goodpush’s estimate), never a fitted parameter.

Steady stretches are not readings: each steady ride (>= 30 min, NP / average power <= 1.15, power recorded >= 90 %; its average power and heart rate) and each steady interval rep (>= 5 min, power within 12 %; heart rate from its settled second half) goes in as power at its heart rate, with heart rate as a share of threshold heart rate between 0.55 and 1.1. The model reads them through your own heart-rate curve (Likelihood). Until 2026-11-09 each was scaled to threshold heart rate along a straight line from resting heart rate; that read 0 % low from easy rides and about 11 % low from tempo rides, so a week of different riding looked like a change in CP.

Recovery markers (sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, bedtime) are built but not used by the model; why is in Design choices and limits. They act through the plan’s day-to-day readiness instead.
