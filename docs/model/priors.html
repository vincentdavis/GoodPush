Priors¶

Every parameter has an independent Normal prior on its unconstrained scale (log for positive quantities, logit for shares), so the log posterior can be minimised and sampled directly on one flat vector (goodpush.physio.model.ParamSpec, values in goodpush.physio.PhysioModel.blocks()). Below: the prior’s median on the natural scale and its 90 % range.

MAP and Pmax are centred on the rider’s own first year of rides (at least 10 with efforts, else the generic watts): in fixed watts the priors sat two to three standard deviations from a much weaker or stronger rider. Each block of 8 weeks gives its best 5 minutes and 5 seconds, and the centre is their median: most 8-week blocks hold a near all-out effort, and a block a broken power meter set moves no median (the year’s best did: one minutes-long 1000 W reading put a synthetic rider’s MAP centre at 3.3 x the truth). The ranges stay as wide as before.

The floors are what the states decay toward without training; for Pmax and durability (no detraining) they are prior only. The CP / MAP prior is the narrow one on purpose: with a wider one (0.57 - 0.89) the fit can explain every effort as well below a huge, never-tested ceiling, as well as with the real one.

The training effects are deliberately vague (log sd 1.66, a factor of 15 either way at 90 %): which domain trains what, and how much, is what the model is meant to learn from the data. The pattern of the gain matrix (which entries may be non-zero) is fixed. Except MAP’s effects from work between CP and MAP (VO2max intervals) and above MAP (log sd 0.6): a rider’s minutes there always come together with their hours, so the data can’t tell those effects apart, and with the wide prior they slid to almost nothing (one athlete’s data, 2026). They are held near what studies of trained cyclists found (about 0.05 - 0.18 % of MAP per 10 minutes between CP and MAP, at a detraining time constant of 200 - 300 days; Rønnestad et al., 2014, Seiler et al., 2013, Stöggl and Sperlich, 2014), and shown as assumed when the draws stay at the prior.

LT1’s share of 1-h power has a narrower time constant (log sd 0.4, about 27 - 130 days at 95 %) than MAP and CP / MAP (0.7). After complete cessation of training the threshold’s share of the ceiling falls slowly and only partly, and has levelled within 8 - 12 weeks: in endurance athletes who stopped, submaximal responses changed over the first 56 days and then held, and after 84 days the lactate threshold still sat at a higher share of VO2max than in untrained people (Coyle et al., 1985, Coyle et al., 1984; reviewed by Mujika and Padilla, 2000, Mujika and Padilla, 2000). Everyday rides say little about this constant (a rider rarely stops for long), so with the wider prior the data left it wider than the prior, and the sampler stalled in its long tail.

Every source has a fitted bias, so the level of a threshold is set by the balance of these bias priors and the best powers: a source with a tight bias prior (your own FTP entries, ramp tests) anchors it most.

Tests are the reference where they measure a threshold well, and a ramp measures its thresholds from above: its LT1 and LT2 read high (alpha1 and heart rate lag the rising power), so they are soft upper bounds (Likelihood) - the threshold is at most the reading, with about 3.5 % slack (5 % from heart rate), and no bias of their own beyond a 1 % tolerance. The ramp’s top is not a MAP reading: legs can give out before the aerobic ceiling, after all the ramp has taken, so it can sit well below fresh 5-minute power. The ride’s best powers enter as efforts instead (a test may be all-out or not). A fit made with other weights is redone, starting from it (PhysioRun.priors).

A ramp LT2 at 92 % or more of the ramp’s top, or within 3 bpm of the ride’s highest heart rate, crossed only at exhaustion (RampTest.lt2_check): as a bound it is loose and harmless, the pages say so, and it is no FTP value for the fitted FTP model.

Fixed, not fitted¶

Some quantities are constants because fitting them breaks the fit (each was tried):

  • the all-out scatter, 3 % (fitted, it widens until “all-out” describes ordinary rides);

  • the share of best powers that are bad readings, \(10^{-4}\), spread over 2 in log power (fitted, it would explain ordinary rides away);

  • the process-noise size per state (fitted, the mode runs to large noise that matches every reading);

  • the minimum deficit shape, 2 (below it the likelihood has infinite density or gradient at the curve);

  • the soft domain-edge width, 5 % in log power;

  • hard physical limits on the states (MAP 40-1500 W, LT1 / 1-h power 0.2-0.98, CP/MAP 0.4-0.98, Pmax 100-3000 W, fade 0.005-1), which keep wild optimiser trial points finite; nothing real is near them.
